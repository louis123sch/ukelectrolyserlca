already have. Sources confirmed against NESO **FES 2025 Economics Assumptions
v1** (published 2025-12-11), which is a signposting document — it names the
authoritative UK source for each assumption rather than tabulating values.

| **Bought-hydrogen road haulage** | Compression onto the trailer is now charged; **the journey is not**. `ASSUMPTIONS.md` §3 used to exclude the whole delivery leg on the grounds that production is co-located with use — true of hydrogen this study makes, false by definition of hydrogen it buys. | **Hydrogen loading compressor capex** | The compression **carbon** term is complete; the **cost** term is electricity only, at `PRODUCER_ELEC_GBP_PER_MWH`. The cost side is therefore a floor and a residual bias toward buying remains — smaller than before, and now in one place rather than two |
| **Bought-hydrogen outlet pressures** | Green's 30 bar is read from `electrolysers.DELIVERY_PRESSURE_BAR`, which is sourced. Turquoise is the weakest of the three: molten-media methane pyrolysis is generally near-atmospheric, which would make its compression **2.3x larger**. | **Electrolyser stack/BoP split** | Hermesmann's capital good is one monolithic activity, so stack replacement cannot be charged separately and the whole capital is amortised on calendar life. | **Battery standing loss** | Self-discharge plus auxiliary/HVAC load, `battery.STANDING_LOSS_PER_HOUR`. Zero and **assumed**, now stated in the module rather than supplied by a caller's `getattr` default against a constant that had never been defined — the model ran a loss-free battery and nothing said so. Not negligible in principle: auxiliary load moves system RTE by 2–4 points seasonally and bites hardest on a lightly cycled battery, which is what the minimum-carbon builds hold (below 1 % of capacity for 5,540 of 8,675 hours on the 300 MW Highland run). Modo's 88 % is a round-trip figure and carries no standing term, so a value cannot be taken from it. This and the tank's leakage are the same parameter on opposite sides of the hydrogen-versus-battery seasonal contest, and both are currently zero. |
| **Alkaline delivery pressure** | `electrolysers.DELIVERY_PRESSURE_BAR` is sourced for PEM (30 bar, the commercialised class) and SOEC (atmospheric), but **assumed** at 30 bar for alkaline, which is deployed both ways and whose source inventory does not state which. If the AE inventory is atmospheric, its compression parasitic is understated by ~2.1 kWh/kg, the same error SOEC carried. |
| **Fuel cell duty cost** | Found by `audit.py`. `dc_model.gwp` charges the fuel cell's stack and BoP on the convex `max(calendar, duty)` replacement rule, so running it hard buys extra replacements *in carbon*. `h2_capex.fuel_cell_costs()` annualises capex over the BoP calendar life alone with a flat CRF, so the same extra duty is **free in cost**. The two objectives are charging different physics for one machine, and the bias is one-directional: cost-optimal builds will over-run the fuel cell. Fixing it needs a £/MWh or per-replacement stack cost. |
| **Electrolyser consumables cost** | Same shape, smaller. `ely_variable_gwp_per_kg` charges consumables carbon on `p_ely`; nothing charges their cost. |
| **Electrolyser consumables cost** | Found by `audit.py`. Water and heat are charged in carbon (`variable_kgco2e_per_kg`) and not in cost, because no price for either is sourced here. Material and one-directional: SOEC draws **18.864 MJ (5.24 kWh) of external heat and 0.65 m³ of water per kg H₂**, against PEM's 0.28 kWh and 0.10 m³. SOEC's electricity advantage over PEM is 21.7 %; on **total energy** it is 12.4 %. The model therefore selects SOEC partly on heat it neither pays for nor checks is available — and at ~800 °C, a data centre's own waste heat cannot supply it. |
